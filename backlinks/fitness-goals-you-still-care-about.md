## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I set a fitness goal that I will actually stick with for more than a few months?

I've coached competitive athletes for ten years. Honestly, most goals fail because of how they're built, not because the person lacks discipline. Around week eight or nine the early excitement wears off. Whatever's left has to carry you, and a vague goal carries nothing.

Here's what I look for in a goal that lasts:

1. It's about something you can do. 'Lose 15 pounds' or 'get in shape' tells you nothing on most days. 'Deadlift my bodyweight', 'run 5k under 28 minutes' or 'do 5 strict pull-ups' does. Every session shows you whether you're closer.

2. It has a date. A real one, ideally tied to something: a race, a test day with your coach, a trip. 'By summer' is not a date. About twelve weeks before a fixed day works for most people.

3. It runs on a weekly habit, not willpower. Decide what a normal training week looks like, for example three strength sessions and one long walk, and make hitting that week your real target. Willpower runs out. After a couple of months, a routine you've repeated mostly runs itself.

A quick test: if you had a bad week, would your goal tell you exactly what to do next Monday? If not, rewrite it.

This doesn't mean weight doesn't matter. Body composition often improves as a side effect of chasing performance. People also tend to stick around long enough to see that change, because every week of work gives them something to show for it.

I wrote a longer breakdown of this, including how I rework weight-loss goals with clients: https://siavashsed.github.io/fitpulsepro/fitness-goals-you-still-care-about/

Start with one performance goal, one date and one weekly routine. That's enough to carry you past the point where most people quit.
