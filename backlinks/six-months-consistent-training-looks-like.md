## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How long does it take to see real results from consistent training?

I've coached competitive athletes for about ten years, and the honest answer is that it takes longer than most people expect, and the early weeks are deceptive.

In the first four to eight weeks, most of the change is neurological. Your body gets better at recruiting muscle, your movement patterns clean up, your recovery between sessions improves, and your work capacity creeps up. You may feel better and perform better, but the mirror and the scale often show very little. This is exactly where most people decide it isn't working and quit.

The visible changes, like body composition, noticeable muscle, and clear strength gains, typically show up in months four through six. That assumes you're training three or so times a week, eating reasonably, sleeping enough, and progressing your loads over time.

I once coached a 34-year-old rower who showed up three mornings a week at 6:15 in a cold gym. At week eight his weight hadn't moved a pound and he wanted to stop. But his splits and lifts were improving steadily. He stayed, and by month six the change was obvious to everyone.

A few practical tips:

1. Track performance, not just weight. Reps, times, loads and how you feel are better early indicators.
2. Take progress photos or measurements monthly rather than weekly, since day-to-day noise hides the trend.
3. Build the habit first. A boring, repeatable schedule beats an ambitious one you abandon in week five.
4. Expect a plateau around weeks six to twelve and plan to push through it, not around it.
