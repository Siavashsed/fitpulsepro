## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much protein per day do you actually need to build muscle?

Short answer: for most lifters, about 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight per day is enough. That's roughly 0.73 grams per pound, not the one gram per pound rule you'll hear in every gym.

I've coached competitive athletes for ten years, and I learned this the expensive way. In 2019 I had a 24-year-old collegiate rower eating 2.6 g/kg because I assumed more was safer. She spent around $140 a month on powder and her progress didn't improve over athletes eating far less.

Here's how I'd approach it in practice:

1. Work out your target. Multiply your body weight in kilograms by 1.6. A 80 kg lifter lands around 128 grams a day.

2. Spread it out. Three to four meals with a solid serving of protein each works well, and there's no need to obsess over exact timing.

3. Prioritize whole foods first. Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes and tofu cover most of it. Powder is a convenience, not a requirement.

4. Know when to go higher. If you're in an aggressive calorie deficit, pushing toward 2.0 to 2.2 g/kg can help hold on to lean mass. That's a specific situation, not a default.

5. Check the other levers before adding more protein. If you've stalled, look at training volume, progressive overload, sleep, and total calories. Those explain far more stalled progress than protein ever does.

The main takeaway is that you don't need to chase extreme numbers. Hit a reasonable target consistently and put your effort into training.
