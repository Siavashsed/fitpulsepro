## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What should I actually track in my workout log to keep making progress?

Track the numbers that decide your next workout. Everything else is optional.

I've coached competitive athletes for ten years. The most common logging mistake I see is recording plenty, just not the right thing. When I took on 14 collegiate throwers in spring 2019, 11 of them had tidy notebooks full of entries I couldn't act on. '140 kg squat, 3x5' tells me what happened. It doesn't tell me whether that was easy or a grinder, so it can't tell me what to do next.

For every working set, log four things:

1. Load: the weight on the bar.
2. Reps: what you actually completed, not what was planned.
3. Sets: so you can see your total work.
4. Reps in reserve (RIR): how many more clean reps you could have done. Most people skip this one, and it's the most useful.

RIR is what turns your log into a decision tool. A simple rule: if you finished every set with 2 to 3 reps left, add a small amount of load next time. If you had 1 left, repeat the weight. If you had 0 or missed reps, hold or drop slightly.

A few practical tips:
- Write it down right after the set. An RIR you guess at an hour later is fiction.
- Be honest. Inflating RIR to justify a jump catches up with you fast.
- Skip RIR on warmups. Working sets are what count.
- Look at your last two or three sessions before you train, not after.

Notes on sleep, mood or how the warmup felt are fine if they help you. If they never change a decision, though, they're decoration.
