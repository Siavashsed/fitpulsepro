## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I skip a hard workout if my Whoop recovery score is low?

Not automatically. A low recovery score is a reason to check in with your body, not an order to skip.

Here is what the number actually is. Whoop combines heart rate variability, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and sleep data into a 0 to 100 score. A sustained drop over several days is worth taking seriously. A single low morning tells you much less.

The problem is that the sensor sees that your HRV fell, not why. Poor sleep in an unfamiliar bed, a large late meal, alcohol, dehydration, a stressful workday and genuine training fatigue all push the same inputs in the same direction. Only one of those means you cannot handle a hard session.

Sensor noise matters too. Strap fit, wrist placement and how the algorithm treats a restless night can all shift the score, so a few points either way is not a meaningful signal.

A practical way to decide:

1. Look at the trend, not the day. Three or more low days in a row, especially with a rising resting heart rate, is a real flag.
2. Do your normal warm-up and judge it. If the weights move well and you feel sharp, the score was probably reacting to something incidental.
3. Use a performance check. Bar speed, pace at a familiar effort, or how a known warm-up set feels is more direct than any wrist metric.
4. Adjust instead of cancel. Keep the session, trim volume or top-end intensity, and reassess tomorrow.

If you feel sick, are in pain, or the trend has been sliding for a week, rest. Otherwise, let the score inform the decision rather than make it.
