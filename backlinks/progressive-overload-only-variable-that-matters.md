## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why have I stopped making strength gains after several years of lifting, and what should I change?

Short answer: you've probably stopped asking your body for anything new.

I've coached competitive athletes for ten years. The most common plateau I see doesn't come from laziness or bad genetics. It comes from being consistent without progressing. People find a routine that works, keep showing up, and slowly stop changing the numbers. In your first year, nearly any training builds strength because the stimulus is new. By year three or five, your body has adapted to what you do, so repeating it only maintains what you have.

The fix is progressive overload. You gradually increase the demand on a muscle and track it, so you know it's actually happening. That doesn't only mean adding weight. You can progress by:

- Adding a rep or two at the same load before you go heavier (double progression)
- Adding a set to raise your weekly volume
- Shortening your rest periods
- Improving range of motion or slowing the lowering phase
- Training a lagging lift more often

Pick one or two of these for each training block, not all of them at once. Then write everything down. If you can't point to something measurable that improved over the last six to eight weeks, you've found your problem.

Here's a real example. I took over coaching a 400m sprinter who had done the same circuit for three seasons, including goblet squats with a 24kg bell for 3x10. Their attendance was great, but the work hadn't asked anything new of them in years.
