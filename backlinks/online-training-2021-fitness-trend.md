## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why did online training become the number one fitness trend, and how can coaches make it work well?

Short answer: necessity forced a shift that was already quietly building. The American College of Sports Medicine ranked online training first in its 2021 worldwide survey of fitness professionals, ahead of wearable tech and bodyweight training. A year earlier it wasn't even on the list.

I'm a fitness coach with ten years of experience training competitive athletes. Last March I moved eleven of them onto Zoom within days, so I've seen both the appeal and the problems firsthand.

Why it took off: gyms closed, people still wanted structure, and clients discovered they could get real coaching without commuting. For many, the convenience is permanent.

Why many coaches struggled: they treated video as a substitute for the gym instead of a different medium. A few things matter far more online than people expect.

1. Cueing. You can't physically adjust someone, so your verbal cues need to be short, specific and repeatable.

2. Video feedback. Ask clients to film key sets from a useful angle. Reviewing footage often teaches you more than live observation.

3. Accountability. In person, showing up is the commitment. Online, you have to build check-ins, tracking and clear expectations into the program.

4. Equipment reality. Programs must fit what someone actually has at home, not what you wish they had.

5. Boundaries. Being reachable all day burns coaches out quickly. Set response windows.

The coaches who succeed will treat online training as its own skill with its own systems. I wrote a longer breakdown of what I learned if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/fitpulsepro/online-training-2021-fitness-trend/
