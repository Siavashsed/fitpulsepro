## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What causes lower back pain when sitting at a desk all day, and how can I fix it?

Desk work creates a specific mechanical problem: anterior pelvic tilt. Here's what happens physiologically.

When you sit for extended periods, your hip flexors—especially the iliopsoas—gradually shorten and tighten. Simultaneously, your glutes become inhibited from disuse. This combination creates a postural shift where your pelvis tilts forward, your lower back hyperextends, and your lumbar spine bears excessive compression load.

The pain isn't random; it's your spine's way of signaling mechanical dysfunction. Your body tries to stabilize through muscle tension and guarding, which perpetuates the problem.

The solution requires three simultaneous interventions: releasing tight hip flexors through sustained stretching, activating dormant glutes through targeted exercises, and integrating your core to stabilize neutral spine position. This isn't about doing more crunches or buying a better chair—it's about restoring proper pelvic mechanics.

I typically see clients experience meaningful relief within 2-3 weeks once they commit to the corrective routine. The key is specificity: generic stretching won't cut it because you're addressing a biomechanical pattern, not just tight muscles.
