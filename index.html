1. Strength Training Fundamentals

Progressive overload isn't optional—it's the law. If you're not tracking your lifts and systematically increasing volume or intensity over time, you're leaving gains on the table. The compound movements (squat, deadlift, bench, row, overhead press) should form the foundation of any serious program.

💪 No-BS Principle

Stop chasing the pump. Focus on getting stronger in the 4-8 rep range on compound lifts. Hypertrophy follows strength. The bodybuilders who look the most impressive are almost always the strongest in the gym.

Program Selection: What Actually Matters

What Works

  • Consistent training frequency (3-5 days/week)
  • Progressive overload tracked in a logbook
  • Compound movements as program foundation
  • Adequate protein intake (0.7-1g per lb bodyweight)

What's Wasting Your Time

  • Program hopping every 2 weeks
  • Chasing soreness as a progress indicator
  • Overcomplicating periodization as a beginner
  • Spending more on supplements than food

2. Cardiovascular Conditioning

Cardio doesn't kill gains—poor programming does. Zone 2 work builds your aerobic base and improves recovery between sets. High-intensity intervals improve VO2 max and metabolic conditioning. Both have their place depending on your goals.

Stop skipping cardio because some influencer said it's catabolic. A well-conditioned lifter recovers faster, performs better, and lives longer. Do 150 minutes of moderate cardio per week minimum. Your heart is also a muscle.
